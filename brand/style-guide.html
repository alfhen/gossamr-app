The idea

Gossamer is the fine silk of a spider's web. Gossamr holds the many fine threads of a person's work (tickets, branches, agents, pull requests) in one light, strong web, with Pip at the centre.

Calm
A tool a person lives in all day. Nothing shouts, blinks red or begs for attention.
Quiet
One line where one line will do. Pip explains the rest when asked.
Precise
Exact words, the same word for the same thing, and claims that are true.
A little playful
Small and earned: Pip blinks, and cobwebs gather on tickets nobody has touched.

Logo

A silk G, with Pip, a small teal spider, hanging into it on a hairline thread. Pip is the only bright point. Below 64 px, use the small version: the G with Pip as a single dot. The app icon is the one place the mark takes the silk-glass gloss: a frosted G with a bright edge on ink, and Pip drawn as a dew bead.

Wordmark

"Gossamr": one word, capital G, no "e" before the final r. System sans, semibold, the mark to its left.

Don't

  • Recolour the G or Pip, add gloss, outlines or shadows, or rotate the mark. The app icon is the only exception to gloss.
  • Use the full mark below 64 px: Pip's legs and thread blur.
  • Separate Pip from its thread, or hang Pip anywhere but inside the G.
  • Use gold or amber anywhere in the brand. Amber is the app's warning colour.
  • Replace the wordmark's G with the mark.

Colour

Ink-indigo, with one teal character. Teal means Pip. Indigo means you. State colours mean state, never decoration.

Type

The Mac's own faces. The app reads in system-ui (SF Pro Text). Headings on the site and in this guide are set in ui-rounded (SF Pro Rounded), whose round strokes match the silk G and Pip. ui-monospace is for code, keys and ids. Regular and semibold. Sentence case everywhere, no all-caps labels.

Space, shape and depth

A 4 px step
Rows and controls mostly use 8 px gaps and 8 to 12 px side padding.
Corners by size
round on controls, pills and avatars; 6 to 7 px on rows; 10 px on cards; 14 to 16 px on sheets.
Lines, not boxes
Hairline separators. A border only where something is a distinct object: a card, a field, a draft.
Shadow means floating
Popovers, the palette, toasts and Pip's launcher cast a shadow. Controls sit just above their surface with a small one. Lists and cards lie flat.
Controls are silk glass
Button, ButtonGroup, Segmented, Switch and SearchField in src/components/. Clear glass by default, indigo for the one main action, Pip's colour on the label for Ask Pip. Heights 22, 28 and 32 px, fully rounded, 12 px labels at medium weight. Inside a glass surface they are lenses; with Reduce transparency they are solid.

The app is compact, like a Mac utility. The site breathes: 96 to 128 px between sections on desktop, 64 px on phones.

Motion

Two speeds. The interface moves quickly and quietly, so you never wait for it. Pip and the cobweb spiders move slowly and gently, because they are alive.

Principles

  1. Motion explains where things are.The peek slides in from the right because it lives there. A menu drops from its button.
  2. Quick for the interface, slow for life.Interface motion is done within 220 ms. Pip and the spiders take seconds.
  3. One thing moves at a time.No staggered lists, no cascades, no several regions at once.
  4. A loop means working.Only work in progress loops. When the work stops, the motion stops. Pip's slow idle life is the one exception.
  5. No bounce in the interface.Overshoot and swing belong to Pip and the spiders.
  6. Never move what someone is reading.The timeline holds its place, and counts change without animating.
  7. Motion is never the only signal.Every state is also in words or colour, so turning motion off loses nothing.

Pip's vocabulary

  • Blink at the end of each 5 s cycle; leg twitches on 7 s and 9 s cycles, so Pip never repeats exactly.
  • Eyes follow the pointer in 120 ms. No waving, no jumping, no motion on new messages.
  • Cobweb spiders rest 5 to 18 s, then drop (2.4 s), climb (2 s), swing (2.2 s), turn (1.6 s), twitch (0.7 s) or crawl to another spot.
  • With reduced motion everything stands still, and Pip pauses whenever the window is hidden.
Proposal: silk glass is built, the rest is not

Dew and silk glass

Dew gathers on the threads, each bead pulled round by surface tension. When a bead grows heavy it lets go, and the thread springs back. Gossamr's drafts could move the same way, on surfaces of clear silk glass inspired by Liquid Glass.

Springs instead of curves

A spring keeps its speed when it is interrupted and comes to rest the way real things do. Each is written as a CSS linear() easing sampled from the spring.

dew, 340 ms, no overshoot
silk, 770 ms, 4%
drop, 1.07 s, 12.5%

Which spring where

Dew: sheets, popovers, the palette, hover. It is done by 220 ms and never bounces, so the interface rules hold.

Silk: floating glass, Pip's pane, toasts. A give you feel more than see.

Drop: Pip, a bead forming, a thread springing back. The only visible bounce, kept for living things.

Fall: cubic-bezier(0.11, 0, 0.5, 0), gravity. Slow to let go, then faster and faster.

Silk glass

For what floats above the work: the palette, menus, toasts, Pip's pane header and the site's navigation. Lists, cards and reading text stay solid. Glass is for controls, never content.

Dark
  • Body: white at 22% (ink at 38% in dark) with a faint lavender tint. Blur 6 to 8 px, saturation up, so shapes and colour come through.
  • Rim: a 1 px light, brightest at the top-left corner, fading along the sides, glowing again at the bottom-right. Plus a soft inner light along the top.
  • Selection is a clearer lens inside the glass, with its own rim. Text is full-strength ink, medium weight.
  • Pip's launcher is not glass: Pip hangs free on its thread over the glass and the work, with only its soft violet shadow.
  • If text can't keep 4.5:1 over the worst content behind it, raise the body (white 55%, ink 60%) or go solid. Reduce transparency makes it solid.
  • No refraction in the app: WebKit doesn't apply SVG filters in backdrop-filter. On the site, refraction is an enhancement for browsers that do, on the hero and navigation only.

How to get there

  1. Tokens: add the springs and the fall to theme.css, and move the popover and peek motions onto dew.
  2. Dew: approve as a falling bead, skip as evaporation, the badge swell, the ripple.
  3. Silk glass: the palette and menus, then toasts and Pip's pane header. Measure scrolling with a menu open first.
  4. Threads: a hairline from Pip to each new draft, and menus that flow from their buttons.
  5. gossamr.app: Pip drops into the G on the drop spring, glass navigation, refraction in the hero.

With reduced motion, none of this moves: a bead doesn't fall, the card is simply gone and Undo says so. Glass stays, because it doesn't move.

Pip

Gossamr's one character: a round teal spider with two eyes, friendly rather than insect-like.

  • In the app, Pip is the animated avatar, drawn as a dew bead: a teal-to-violet body with a soft highlight and a white rim that catches light, a smaller bead for a head, a small smile, eight thin legs, and eyes whose pupils carry a catchlight and follow the pointer. Gloss belongs to Pip's bead and the app icon only; the flat mark stays flat.
  • In text, Pip has its own glyph: a spider on its thread, with a bead and two eyes cut out of it. It takes the colour of the text around it and is 1em tall, so it stands where a symbol would: the Pip button in the rail, Pip's drafts and their badges, "Ask Pip" in the palette, on a text selection, on the Work and ticket buttons and in a run result, and the "Pip asks you" box. The four-point spark stays only for a run that is working. It replaced the four-point star (✦), which says "AI" on every product that uses it. Gossamr never sells itself as AI, so what marks Pip's work is Pip.
  • In the brand, Pip is the flat mark: flat teal, two eyes, four legs.
  • Pip appears where Pip acts or speaks: the launcher, the pane, Pip's drafts. Never as decoration, and never on errors as a joke.
  • Pip is "Pip": never "the AI", "the bot" or "the assistant" in the interface.

Voice

A careful colleague: plain, short and exact. Address the person as "you". Use the app's own words the same way every time: workstream, agent, draft, Needs you, Inbox, Work, Board, List.

  • One idea per sentence, in the active voice.
  • One line per empty state. No explainers, no glossary.
  • Two facts are said once, in Settings: Gossamr posts nothing to Jira or GitHub until you approve it, and agents run with your own Claude Code settings.
  • One status word per workstream: Needs you, Failed, Running, Done or Idle.
  • Buttons are verbs that name the result. Errors say what failed and what to do next.
  • Avoid "magic", "supercharge", "seamless", "effortless", "AI-powered", exclamation marks, and emoji in the interface.

gossamr.app

The app's front door: the app's colours, type and voice, and the real app. The top of this page shows the hero.

  • Dark first. The hero sits on ink, with the mark and the wordmark, as at the top of this page. Pip, inside the mark, is the hero's one teal thing. Light sections follow for reading.
  • One teal thing per screen, and it is Pip. Calls to action are indigo.
  • Real screenshots in both themes, each cropped to one idea: a draft card, Needs you, Pip's pane.
  • True, plain claims: drafts are the gate, the cache works offline, agents use your own Claude Code setup, Jira and GitHub on macOS. Linear only once it ships.
  • Motion: Pip may bob once on its thread as the page loads. Nothing else moves on scroll. Respect reduced motion.
  • Layout: content at most 1120 px, text at most 680 px, 16 px gutters on phones, no sideways scrolling.
  • Favicon: the small mark. Social image: the full mark on ink beside the wordmark.
  • Accessibility: AA contrast for all text, a visible indigo focus ring, alt text that says what a screenshot shows.
